A heart disease risk model, deployed as a web app anyone can try

A classifier that predicts whether heart disease is present from 13 routine clinical measurements. It is a small learning project, and the first one I took all the way from raw data to a live app.

The problem

Given measurements such as age, blood pressure, cholesterol, chest pain type and exercise test results, predict whether a patient has heart disease. The data is the classic Statlog heart dataset: 270 patients, with no missing values.

How I built it

  • Exploration. Distributions, class balance, correlations and how each feature differs between patients with and without disease.
  • One pipeline. Imputing, scaling and encoding are wrapped together with the model in a scikit-learn Pipeline, so the app applies the same steps as training did.
  • Two models. Logistic regression as a baseline and a Ridge classifier, each tuned with randomised search and cross-validation.

Read these with care. The test set has only 54 patients, so one wrong prediction moves accuracy by about 2 points. The cross-validation score of about 0.81 is the more reliable estimate.

What drives the prediction

The strongest signals in the final model were the number of major vessels seen on fluoroscopy, the thallium stress test result, chest pain type, sex and ST depression during exercise. Age had a small negative weight, which goes against medical knowledge and is most likely an effect of the small sample.

Deployment and limits

The trained pipeline is saved to a file and loaded by a Streamlit app hosted on Streamlit Community Cloud. You enter the measurements and get a prediction with lifestyle tips.

This is a learning project. It is not a medical device and its output is not medical advice. It was trained on 270 records and leaves out lifestyle and genetic factors.
