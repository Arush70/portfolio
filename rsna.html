RSNA 2026: detecting knee abnormalities in MRI scans

A Kaggle medical imaging competition. I trained vision models on knee MRI volumes, found that labels mattered more than architecture, and found a bug in a widely used public feature recipe.

Two approaches

  • My own model. A DINOv2-small vision transformer fine-tuned on MRI volumes I built myself: 5 views of 20 slices each. It scored about 0.80 on the public leaderboard.
  • A frozen public encoder with my own head. I ran a public DINOv3 model once over every study, cached a small feature vector, and trained only a small classifier on top. Training then took minutes on a laptop instead of hours.

Things I found

A bug in a public recipe. While reproducing a popular public feature pipeline, I found its patch tokens were taken one position too early. That made the grid non-square, and its "focal" feature silently became a copy of the mean. My version uses the correct offset.
  • Labels were the biggest lever. Mixing in better public labels helped every metric, more than any model change.
  • Scanner type leaks the answer. The scanner fingerprint alone predicts the labels at about 0.65 AUC. So I grouped cross-validation folds by scanner, or validation scores would have been inflated.
  • Fine-tuning overfits fast. End-to-end fine-tuning peaked at epoch 2 or 3 and then declined.
